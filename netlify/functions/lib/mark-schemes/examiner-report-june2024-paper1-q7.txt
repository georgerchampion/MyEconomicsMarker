=== EXAMINER COMMENTARY ON QUESTION 7 ===

"In this question, candidates were asked to evaluate the microeconomic effects of rising
energy bills on the hotel industry or an industry of the candidates' choice. Inevitably, most
responses focussed on the hotel industry because the stem provided a location, a specific
real world example, and a pathway into a discussion about the buying power of larger
hotels. The responses to this question tended to follow the impact of cost and quality of
provision, as hotels reduced services in a struggle to balance the books. There were many
imaginative responses which discussed the differential impact depending on location, time
of year, and whether the hotel was small or part of a large chain. Again, the good use of
accurately drawn cost and revenue diagrams often significantly enhanced the quality of many
responses. Short run shutdown scenarios were particularly effective."

=== REAL GRADED EXAMPLE 1 — SCORED 11/25 ===
Two KAA paragraphs. First: rising energy bills reduce profits, reducing hotels' ability to
expand — awarded Level 2. Evaluative point: hotels could raise prices, but this depends on
price elasticity of demand of customers — awarded Level 2 evaluation. Second KAA point:
falling customer demand affecting the industry because of scale of cost increase — not well
developed, awarded Level 2, no further evaluation.
RESULT: Level 2 KAA = 7 marks, Level 2 evaluation = 4 marks. Total 11/25.

Examiner's generalised advice from this example: "Writing a 25 mark essay is a crucially
important skill. There should be at least two KAA paragraphs to develop both the depth and
breadth of argument. There should be in depth evaluation of EACH KAA paragraph. At the
end of the essay there should be a conclusion that works towards a justified judgement."

=== REAL GRADED EXAMPLE 2 — SCORED 23/25 ===
First KAA paragraph: rising costs reduce abnormal profits, deepened by a carefully drawn
diagram showing increased AC and MC and reduced profits; explains reduced quality of
service and potential job losses — awarded Level 4. Evaluation: negative impacts depend on
hotel location (location may matter more than service quality) — awarded Level 3
evaluation. Second KAA paragraph: negative impact on competition — smaller hotels can't
absorb cost increases the way bigger hotels can; discussion of possible oligopolistic collusion
leading to higher prices — awarded Level 4. Evaluation: government may take measures to
offset negative effects on competition — awarded Level 2 evaluation (lower, because the
conclusion introduced new, undeveloped material on Airbnb/contestability rather than
synthesising the existing argument).
RESULT: 16 marks KAA + 7 marks evaluation = 23/25.

KEY LESSON FOR FEEDBACK: the single mark lost between an excellent Level 4 answer and a
perfect one was in the CONCLUSION — introducing a brand new, undeveloped point (Airbnb/
contestability) instead of synthesising and prioritising the arguments already made. This is a
precise, specific, checkable failure mode worth flagging whenever an essay's conclusion
introduces new unintegrated material.
